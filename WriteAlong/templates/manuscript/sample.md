# Chapter One

The map arrived on a Tuesday, rolled inside a length of drainpipe and sealed at both ends with candle wax. There was no letter with it, no return address, only Iris's name written on a luggage tag in handwriting she recognised at once and had not seen in eleven years.

She did not open it straight away. She set the tube on the kitchen table, made a pot of tea she did not drink, and sat looking at it while the light outside turned from grey to gold to grey again. Her grandfather had taught her that a map was a promise, and that you should never accept a promise without first deciding whether you could bear to keep it.

When at last she cut the wax and slid the paper out, it unrolled across the table with a sound like a long breath. It was a chart of the coast she had grown up on, every cove and headland drawn in his fine brown ink. But it was wrong. There was an island off Carrow Point where no island had ever been, and a road that ran out to it across the water.

---

The ferryman at Carrow remembered her. He was older and heavier, and he walked now with a stick, but he looked at her across the counter of the harbour office as though she had stepped off the boat only yesterday.

"You'll be wanting the old man's cottage," he said. It was not a question.

"I'll be wanting to know why he sent me this." She laid the map in front of him.

He studied it for a long time, tracing the coastline with one thick finger. When he reached the island, his finger stopped, and he did not look up.

"He was out there every night that last winter," he said quietly. "Rowing. We told him there was nothing to row to."

"And was there?"

The ferryman folded the map with great care, along its old creases, and pushed it back across the counter.

"Tide turns at four," he said. "If you mean to go, go before it does."
